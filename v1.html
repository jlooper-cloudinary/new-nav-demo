Why this nav is better

  • Four choices instead of six. Header items go from to , and menu links from to . Distinct pages linked drop from to .
  • It follows what the data says. Developers, Partners, Guides and Glossary together produced about 0.2% of Q3 signups. Pricing, the product pages and both CTAs are untouched.
  • Nothing is deleted. Every page still has a home: Resources for Developers, Partners, Docs and Support, the footer for Guides, Glossary and Labs. URLs do not change and internal links to them remain.
  • One home for each thing. Integrations was listed in four menus and Demos in two. Each now lives in one place.
  • It sells image and video. Platform and Solutions carry a tracked contact-sales offer for each. Video is 12% of sales leads where a solution was picked, against 27% for DAM, so this is the gap leadership asked about.
  • It reads like the best B2B product sites. Four pills on the left, Login and two CTAs on the right, and one panel that resizes between menus, the pattern linear.app uses.
  • Mobile gets simpler too. The bar shows one CTA and a menu, and the drawer carries the same four sections.
  • It is testable. One change, a 50/50 holdout, signups per 1,000 sessions as the measure, and Docs as the guardrail.

If you are asked

“Why is Developers not in the top nav? We are a developer company.”

  • It is not gone. Developers is first in Resources › Build and first in Solutions › By team. Image API, Video API, Docs and Community are one hover away.
  • Developers do not come in through the menu. Organic search and direct are 87% of sessions this year, AI assistants another 9%. /developers lands 4,971 sessions a quarter and 57 signups.
  • The 4M developers already in the product use Login and Docs. Docs lands 162,707 sessions and 3,133 signups a quarter, and it stays in Platform and Resources.

“Why did Partners come out? It was only added this year.”

  • The directory is small. /solution-partners drew about 800 users in Q3 2026, 0.014% of the property’s 5.75M active users, and partner pages produced 3 signups.
  • Views rose, depth did not. /partners went from 1,480 views (Q3 2025) to 10,048 (Q3 2026), but time per user fell from 22s to 7s, and key events on the page went from 56 to 67. The directory itself went from 1,582 views (Q4 2025) to 1,927. Check the nav launch date with the web team before you say anything about lift.
  • Partner revenue runs through partner relationships. Partner channels are 34% of channel-tagged new-business MRR in Salesforce ($1.47M), and no nav link produced that.
  • Partners is still in Resources with the portal link, and the pages are unchanged.

“Why strip the nav down?”

  • The goal is conversions. With four items, Contact Sales and Sign Up for Free are the only filled buttons in the bar. The header buttons already produce 27 of 47 tagged contact-sales leads.
  • Intuitive does not mean everything in view. A visitor needs three answers: what you sell (Platform), who it is for (Solutions), how to learn or get help (Resources). Pricing and the CTAs do the rest.
  • Resources lets us talk about Cloudinary beyond the product list and the self-selected use cases, without competing with them for space in the bar.
  • It is a hypothesis with a test: 50/50 holdout, signups per 1,000 sessions, Docs landing sessions as the guardrail.

Linear’s patterns, as used here

Borrowed from linear.app’s header and dropdowns, kept to what a nav can carry.

  • Right-clustered pills. Four items beside Login and the two CTAs, in sentence case, muted until hovered.
  • One floating panel. It resizes between menus instead of opening a new box each time.
  • Title plus two-line description on the items that need explaining, plain links for the rest.
  • Footer strip inside the panel for the one thing we want noticed, which here is the image and video offer.
  • Blur and a hairline border instead of a full-width slab.

Ideas for the Platform menu

  • Drop the two duplicates. Demos now lives in Resources and Integrations in Solutions.
  • Lead with products. Six cards in a 2 × 3 grid (Video, Image, Assets, MediaFlows, Moderation, Cloudinary AI), with Platform overview, Extensibility and Documentation as plain links. Product pages convert at 1.16% against 0.29% for /platform.
  • Use the strip. The footer strip is the one place to say something. Right now it carries the video and image offers.

What I would test before shipping

  1. Add a nav click event. I could not find a nav-click report in GA4, so everything above compares destinations, not clicks. Two weeks of data on the current bar gives a real baseline.
  2. Hold out the change. Show the proposed bar to half of visitors. Primary metric is signups per 1,000 sessions. Guardrails are Documentation landing sessions and the share of /contact leads that are support requests.
  3. Test the promos on their own. Run the Platform strip with and without the video and image offers and compare video and image share of sales leads.
  4. Watch search. Check Search Console clicks to /guides/ and /glossary/ weekly for a month after the footer move.

  • Docs. It has 162,707 landing sessions and 3,133 signups a quarter, about 55 times the signups of the whole /developers section. Existing customers use that header link daily. It heads its own Docs and Community group, appears again in Platform, and the search icon stays.
  • Solutions. It lands 34,787 sessions and 20 signups. It keeps today’s six integrations and is the next candidate after this round.
  • Blog and Customers. Both have near-zero direct signups. I left them in Resources because they assist, and GA4’s landing view cannot show assists.
  • Dropped from the menu, still one click away. SDK quick starts, the MCP server and the app gallery sit on /developers.

How to read these numbers

  • GA4: property “[GA4] main (website+console)”, landing-page report, Jul 1 – Sep 30 2026, key event sign_up_successfully. Path filters match “contains”, so a family such as /guides/ includes every page under it. Pageview and user counts come from the pages-and-screens report for the same dates.
  • Landing pages show where a session started, not which nav link was clicked. A nav click starts from another page. Treat the visitor counts on /developers and /partners as the ceiling for nav use.
  • Salesforce: Lead.Last_Landing_Page__c begins with the path, created Oct 1 2025 – Oct 1 2026. Lead counts and GA4 signups are different populations and are not meant to reconcile. The lead-to-opportunity link is nearly empty (1 of 65,817 homepage-landing leads has a converted opportunity), so I could not trace landing page to pipeline.
  • Opportunity.Amount is labeled MRR in Salesforce. Channel figures cover New Business opportunities created in the same 12 months. 7,798 of 8,734 have no first-touch channel and are mostly self-serve.
  • Solution mix: Lead.Cloudinary_Form_Solution__c on leads with Reason_for_Contact_Us__c = Sales, created Jul 1 – Oct 2 2026. The field is mostly empty before July.
  • CTA split uses Lead.UTM_Web_CTA_Source__c from Sep 16 2026, the agreed measurement start. The field is cut to 20 characters, so “header” means values starting homepage_nav, pricing_page_nav, demos_page_nav or image_product_page_n.
  • support.cloudinary.com sits outside the pages I pulled, so Support has no GA4 row. The nav list was read from the live homepage on Oct 2 2026. The hero video loads from cloudinary.com when the page is allowed to fetch it; otherwise the drawn card shows. Panel copy for the promos and card descriptions is my draft and needs review.
